Why not just use Apple Dictation?

Apple's engine is good. The way you have to use it isn't, and none of it is configurable.

Hold a key to talk

Apple Dictation is toggle-only, and on a MacBook press-and-hold is already Siri's. Quill lets you hold any modifier and speak — or double-tap it to stay hands-free.

It doesn't stop when you click away

Apple Dictation dies the moment you click into another window. Quill keeps going and the words follow your cursor into whatever you clicked.

No thirty-second timeout

Apple cuts you off after thirty seconds of silence, sometimes dropping the last thing you said. Quill stops when you stop.

Words it has no business knowing

Add names, jargon and product names and the recognizer is told about them, so it hears them right the first time rather than being corrected afterwards.

The short version

  • Runs on your Mac. Apple's on-device speech models. No network, no account, nothing uploaded — not your audio, not your text.
  • Small. Around 2 MB, native Swift, roughly 50 MB of memory sitting idle. Not a browser pretending to be an app.
  • Spoken punctuation. Say "period", "comma", "new line" and get the character, because it uses the same models macOS Dictation does.
  • Works where you type. Native apps, Electron apps, browsers and terminals, each by whichever method that app actually accepts.
